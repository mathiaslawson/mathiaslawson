# Mathias Prince Lawson

I study how systems, artificial and biological represent the limits of their own competence: how they estimate whether an answer is likely to be right, and use that estimate to commit, gather more evidence, or defer.

I come to this from neurosymbolic AI: combining neural models with explicit symbolic structure so that a system's reasoning can be inspected rather than trusted on faith. I am now extending the question into computational neuroscience, where decision confidence and metacognition are studied directly.

## Currently

- **[Coverage detection in retrieval-augmented generation](https://github.com/mathiaslawson/rag-coverage-detection)** — constructing held-out knowledge conditions on 2WikiMultiHopQA by removing entities and relations from the graph and retrieval index, then comparing abstention signals (retrieval similarity, model confidence, graph-path existence) with risk–coverage curves and AUROC. The question: can structure tell *no supporting evidence* from *weak supporting evidence* when similarity cannot?
- **Structured retrieval** — extending a production retrieval system from flat vector search toward ontology-linked knowledge graphs, so that retrieved evidence carries explicit relations rather than similarity scores alone.
- **Neural data** — selected for the ACNEI × International Brain Laboratory Neural Modelling Workshop (Nov 2026), working with the IBL's open decision-making data.
- **Early exploration** — whether capabilities in neural networks can be represented as dependent structures rather than independent modules.
